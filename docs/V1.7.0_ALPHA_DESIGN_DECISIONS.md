# V1.7.0-alpha Design Decision Record

**Status:** Candidate for school evaluation  
**Stable release:** `V1.6.0`  
**Candidate files:** `choice_train_V1.7.0-alpha.html`, `.js`, and `.css`  
**Decision date:** 6 November 2026

## Purpose and Boundary

V1.7.0-alpha aims to make ChoiceTrain feel like a coherent school product without changing its established learning pathway or publishing untested work to GitHub Pages. The build is intentionally isolated from `index.html`.

The design is evidence-informed, not clinically validated. No paper establishes one interface, typeface, colour palette, sound, or reward pattern as best for every SEND learner. The candidate therefore makes presentation choices configurable, records the configuration used, and requires observation with actual learners and staff before stable promotion.

## Decision Summary

| Decision | Why | Implementation | Evidence boundary | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Separate teacher, learner, and admin/research workspaces | Each user has a different goal; exposing all controls together increases search and error burden | Teacher has Today, Personalise, Results; learner has a full activity viewport; admin has Overview, Sessions, Learners, EEG, System | This is a usability decision, not a therapeutic claim | Staff task completion, misclicks, time-on-task, browser tests |
| Make Admin destinations focused | A single hydrated research page became very long and could land between sections | Each Admin destination shows only its relevant groups; navigation scrolls below the fixed header after layout settles | This improves navigation but does not validate research interpretation | Destination tests, visual bounds, staff task testing |
| Focus the teacher start flow | Daily sessions should not require navigating research controls | Readiness summary and one-action learner start while all advanced options remain available | Teacher workflow still needs Brackensfield observation | First-session and repeat-session usability protocol |
| Protect learner exit | An ordinary tap should not expose configuration during a session | 1.2-second press-and-hold return; keyboard activation remains immediate | Hold duration may need adjustment for motor access | Accidental exit rate and assisted-access testing |
| Use a calm light default with strong contrast | Improve legibility and reduce the previous dense, dark administrative presentation | Warm neutral canvas, clear hierarchy, larger controls, contrast checks | No colour scheme is universally calming or SEND-friendly | Automated contrast plus individual preference checks |
| Use semantic colour roles | Fixed component and canvas colours made themes inconsistent and changed meaning between workspaces | Staff and learner token contracts cover surfaces, controls, focus, outcomes, rewards, overlays, charts, heatmaps, and task effects | WCAG validates contrast and non-colour cues, not therapeutic benefit from a palette | Token, contrast, runtime-switch, lint, and screenshot checks |
| Offer two font paths and text scaling | Learners differ in visual familiarity and access needs | Atkinson Hyperlegible Next or familiar sans-serif; 90-130% scale | Atkinson was designed for low vision; evidence does not support claiming a special dyslexia cure | Readability observation and preference recording |
| Make motion, sound, and celebration adjustable | Sensory response and motivation vary between learners and by day | Reduced/gentle/full motion; off/low/standard sound; quiet/gentle/lively celebration | Settings are access options, not sensory diagnoses | Per-pupil trial, context notes, stop/break use |
| Add an original train journey reward | Visible progress can support motivation while matching the ChoiceTrain identity | Progress rail, train, stations, simple stars, no copied artwork or wording | Gamification evidence is mixed; rewards must not obscure the task or research signal | Engagement, latency, repeat requests, task accuracy |
| Generate sounds locally | Provide responsive feedback without a network or licensed sound pack | Short Web Audio oscillator cues; tones stop before speech | Sound can distract or distress some learners, so off is always available | Audio overlap, mute, volume and device tests |
| Preserve local-first data | School pilots should not introduce an unreviewed cloud data flow | Existing localStorage and IndexedDB model retained; no analytics or LLM call | Local data still requires school retention, device and export controls | Network test, privacy review, export inspection |
| Record presentation conditions | Performance may be affected by presentation settings | Schema 4 includes `sensory_preferences`; EEG markers add audio/reward onset | Browser timestamps are not hardware triggers | Export tests and later EEG timing validation |

## Workspace Detail

### Teacher

The teacher workspace exposes the minimum daily route first:

1. Select the pupil.
2. Confirm level, task, length, and question count.
3. Review today's context and presentation preferences.
4. Start the learner session.
5. Review or export results.

Personalisation remains a separate teacher task, and results do not compete visually with setup.

### Learner

The learner surface keeps the prompt, target choices, progress, regulation controls, and essential navigation. It removes the teacher/admin shell, fills the dynamic viewport on tablets, and avoids unused page scrolling. The return control is intentionally discreet but remains accessible.

### Admin and Research

The admin workspace uses the whole viewport because longitudinal tables, EEG controls, fidelity records, and exports need more space than a narrow side panel. It retains every advanced control rather than simplifying by deleting research functionality. `Overview`, `Sessions`, `Learners`, `EEG`, and `System` now act as focused destinations, so loading history does not leave the user in a large mixed document or shift the intended landing position.

## Colour-System Rationale

The staff shell and learner themes have separate visual identities but share one semantic language. A token such as `primary`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `reward`, or `focus` keeps the same purpose wherever it appears. Learner themes can change the actual values without changing what those values mean.

All learner themes define canvas, surface, raised surface, text, muted text, borders, controls, primary action, outcome, reward, focus, overlay, shadow, and task-stimulus roles. JavaScript-rendered cue rings, pointer trails, ripples, heatmaps, and charts resolve those roles at draw time, so switching theme updates both DOM and canvas content. Teacher and Admin components use equivalent `--shell-*` roles, and an automated source guard prevents component-level colour literals from bypassing that contract.

Outcome and progress states do not depend on colour alone. Correct and incorrect responses add check/cross cues; selection and current progress use borders and position; labels remain present for warnings and status. High Contrast stays a distinct option rather than forcing every learner into one palette.

## Legibility and Sensory Rationale

WCAG 2.2 informs contrast, focus, and target-size checks. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is bundled locally under its SIL Open Font License and is presented as a high-legibility option. Kuster et al. (2018) found no reading benefit from the Dyslexie typeface over common fonts; Joseph et al. (2022) similarly cautions against assuming a specialist typeface alone improves reading. This supports user choice, spacing, scale, and testing rather than a universal font claim.

Sensory responses in autistic learners are heterogeneous. Stewart et al. (2016) and Chevalier et al. (2022) support considering sensory processing and individual variability, but they do not prescribe ChoiceTrain's exact colours or sounds. Consequently, teachers can reduce or disable motion, sound, and celebration and can record daily context.

## Reward and Sound Rationale

The progress journey uses general principles familiar from successful learning products: short feedback, visible progress, achievable milestones, and a recognisable journey. It does not copy Duolingo characters, artwork, sounds, vocabulary, or screen design.

Lumsden et al. (2016) found that gamification can support motivation in cognitive assessment and training but that efficacy evidence is mixed. Prins et al. (2011) reported motivational value from game elements in working-memory training for children with ADHD. ChoiceTrain therefore keeps rewards lightweight, optional, and separate from the task rule. In research use, the selected reward and audio conditions are exported and event-marked.

## Research Integrity

- Existing Attention Training families and task rules are unchanged.
- Audio and reward onset receive explicit event markers.
- Feedback sounds pause when speech is active.
- Presentation settings are exported so sessions can be stratified or excluded.
- The alpha does not connect to EEG hardware, LSL, a cloud service, or an LLM.
- Claims about learning, transfer, neuroplasticity, or EEG change require a preregistered protocol and appropriate comparison condition.

## Promotion Gates

V1.7.0-alpha must not replace the stable entry point until:

- the complete stable and alpha browser suites pass
- no new external runtime request is introduced
- keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, contrast, desktop, tablet, and mobile checks pass
- staff can complete first-session and repeat-session tasks without developer help
- learner trials confirm that exit protection, text, motion, sound, and rewards are acceptable and adjustable
- exports and local storage receive a school data-protection review
- EEG language and event timing are reviewed by the research team
- known limitations are accepted and explicit approval to promote is recorded

## Technical Validation Record

At the 6 November 2026 review point, 77 Playwright checks passed: 56 stable/archive regressions and 21 alpha-specific checks. The alpha checks include semantic token completeness, staff and learner contrast, runtime-drawn theme switching, focused Admin navigation, reward-disabled manual navigation, mobile containment, and tablet layout. A repeatable 17-view synthetic-data visual audit covers the staff workspaces, active learner states, responsive break/reward states, and every theme.

This technical result does not close the school-evaluation gates above. Physical device, Safari/Silk, assistive-technology, staff, learner, privacy, and research-protocol reviews remain required before promotion.
